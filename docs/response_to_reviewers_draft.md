# Response to Reviewers Draft

28 April 2026

Dear Editor and Reviewers,

Thank you for the careful evaluation of our manuscript, now titled "Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Profilometry for Rough Surfaces: A Pre-Validation Coincidence-Proxy Benchmark on FV-Derived Surface Topographies".

The revision strategy was not to defend the earlier stronger framing. Instead, we narrowed the manuscript to the portion of the evidence that remains technically defensible. In particular, the revised paper now presents the measured-surface branch as a pre-validation internal-consistency benchmark rather than as external metrological validation; it uses matched-bandwidth benchmark-grid roughness as the only primary manuscript basis; it states explicitly that PSD agreement is only a shared-prior diagnostic; it shows that the surviving direct long-wavelength effect is not uniquely quantum once stronger classical controls are included; and it makes the remaining closure requirements explicit, namely independent reference measurements, archival deposition, and reduced experimental validation before any stronger claim is advanced.

No new recalculation was required for this revision letter. The numerical statements retained in the revised manuscript were checked against the current generated artefacts before this response was prepared.

## Reviewer 1: Validation Framing

Comment: The measured-surface study is not a true validation of interferometric performance, but an internal self-consistency loop.

Response: We agree, and the manuscript now says so directly. The title, abstract, introduction, results, limitations, and conclusions were revised to describe the study as a pre-validation screening benchmark rather than a metrological validation study. The revised text no longer presents the measured-surface branch as proof of absolute interferometric accuracy.

## Reviewer 2: Roughness-Bandwidth Consistency

Comment: The roughness comparison mixes bandwidth bases and is therefore not metrologically consistent.

Response: We agree. The revised manuscript now uses matched-bandwidth benchmark-grid roughness as the only primary basis in the main paper. Native-grid roughness is retained only as a harsher diagnostic sensitivity check and is no longer used as a co-equal manuscript-facing ranking basis.

## Reviewer 3: PSD Interpretation

Comment: PSD agreement is overstated because the reference PSD is also the simulator input.

Response: We agree. The revised manuscript now states explicitly that the PSD view is an internal spectral-consistency diagnostic on the benchmark grid and not an independent spectral validation. The failure taxonomy was also expanded to name the shared-prior and bandwidth-mismatch failure modes directly.

## Reviewer 4: Forward-Model Realism

Comment: The coincidence forward model remains too idealized for a metrology paper.

Response: We largely agree. The revised manuscript no longer treats the present coincidence branch as a laboratory-grounded fourth-order interferometer. It now frames the coincidence branch as a proxy model, the rate-model study as a sensitivity control, and the next revision-critical task as experimental replacement of the proxy hierarchy with a grounded transfer function.

## Reviewer 5: Quantum Overclaim

Comment: The direct long-wavelength behaviour is not uniquely quantum if a stronger classical control reproduces the same effect.

Response: We agree. The revised paper no longer presents the direct branch as evidence of unique quantum advantage. The classical two-colour and classical-frontier controls are now used explicitly to narrow the surviving claim to a hybrid architectural result under the current proxy benchmark.

## Reviewer 6: Uncertainty and Generality

Comment: Bootstrap intervals, holdouts, and related summaries are descriptive rather than metrological uncertainty treatment.

Response: We agree. The revised manuscript now classifies these results as descriptive summaries of the fixed simulator/reference dataset rather than as GUM-style uncertainty budgets or inter-instrument reproducibility studies. The conclusions were correspondingly narrowed.

## Reviewer 7: Internal Contradictions

Comment: The earlier manuscript contained contradictory language about truth, validation, and benchmark scope.

Response: We agree, and those contradictions were removed. Terms such as ground truth were replaced with measured benchmark surface language, validation framing was removed from authorship and conclusion-level statements, and the main text now uses a single consistent benchmark-grid-primary interpretation.

## Reviewer 8: Closure Path and Reproducibility

Comment: The manuscript still needs a credible path toward independent reference checking and archival reproducibility.

Response: We agree. The revision now makes these closure steps mandatory rather than optional. The submission package includes a reduced experimental-validation subset and a revision-closure protocol that require independent reference measurement on every core case, together with explicit acknowledgement that the current materials are not yet a DOI-bearing archival validation package.

## Summary of Substantive Revision

The revised manuscript does not argue that the strongest reviewer criticisms were wrong. It responds by removing claims that were too strong, strengthening the classical controls, restricting roughness reporting to matched-bandwidth benchmark-grid descriptors, and making the remaining unmet requirements explicit. The surviving claim is therefore narrower but technically stronger: within the present proxy benchmark, coincidence-derived information remains worth testing primarily as a coarse ambiguity-resolving prior inside a hybrid estimator when benchmark-grid height fidelity is the primary endpoint.

If helpful to the reviewers, a longer internal point-by-point version with exact manuscript line references is also available in the project revision materials.
